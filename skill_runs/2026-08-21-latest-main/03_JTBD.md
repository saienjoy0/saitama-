# JTBD Decision Artifact — Latest Main Spec

Applied skill: `thinking-jobs-to-be-done`

Important: the current source is a product specification plus secondary research, not completed primary customer discovery. Jobs below are **provisional hypotheses** and must not be presented as validated customer truth.

## Primary child job hypothesis

**Job statement**

When something in my home could be better but I do not immediately know what to do, I want just enough help to notice and think without being handed the answer, so I can decide what I want to do and feel that the idea/action was genuinely mine.

**Performer**: child — primary product user; frequency/stakes unknown.

**Forces**
- Push: being told exactly what job to do leaves little room to discover or decide.
- Pull: agency, usefulness, mastery, optional compensation, family response.
- Anxiety: not knowing what to notice, fear of wrong answers, task feeling like homework, adult judgment.
- Habit: wait until adult tells me what to do; follow known chores.

**Competition**
- direct: chore/pocket-money apps and newer services that include child proposals/problem discovery;
- indirect: parent verbal instruction, chore charts, family conversation, school inquiry activities;
- non-consumption: do nothing until asked.

**Outcomes**
- minimize: amount/intensity of outside support required; feeling of being commanded; unnecessary steps;
- maximize: voluntary repeat, independent framing/proposal, ability to apply the thinking to a new situation, concrete recipient value.

## Primary parent job hypothesis

**Job statement**

When I want my child to learn to notice, decide, and act for themselves but I do not have the time or patience to coach every step, I want a lightweight way to provide only the necessary starting support without giving the answer, so my child can practice independent judgment without turning me into a full-time teacher.

**Performer**: parent — leading requester/payer hypothesis; problem frequency and willingness-to-pay unvalidated.

**Forces**
- Push: parent may want independent judgment yet intervene because “telling is faster”; this remains a hypothesis requiring interviews.
- Pull: less coaching design effort, visible independent growth, useful household action.
- Anxiety: extra setup/response burden, AI overreach, child resistance, privacy/safety, monetizing normal household roles.
- Habit: direct instruction, reminders, doing it oneself.

**Competition**
- direct: education/chore apps;
- indirect: verbal reminders, chore charts, allowance systems, parenting techniques, tutoring/education programs;
- non-consumption: accept that independent action develops informally over time.

**Outcomes**
- minimize: parent prompts, repeated instruction, setup/feedback time, conflict;
- maximize: child-led action, confidence that autonomy is real, ease of repeat use.

## Secondary family/grandparent job hypothesis

When I want to stay meaningfully involved in a child’s growth, I want a lightweight way to respond with questions/experience/requests rather than only receive photos, so I can contribute to the child’s next thought or action.

This is secondary and should not displace the child/parent core until evidence shows stronger stakes.

## Priority implication

**Reposition discovery around the progress being bought, not the feature set.**

Do not position the core as “AI chores,” “problem discovery,” “family newspaper,” or “financial education.”

The highest-priority job gap to validate is the parent-child pair’s need for **independent judgment with less adult answer-giving** and the child’s ability to experience the resulting action as self-authored rather than another guided assignment.

## Countercase checked

Strong countercase:

> Parents may not experience “I want autonomy but I lack time to scaffold it” as a frequent or painful problem. Direct instruction may be good enough, and children may prefer clear chores/rewards over ambiguous problem discovery.

If primary interviews show parents rarely care enough to search, spend, or change behavior around this problem, the current parent-payer JTBD is wrong even if the educational philosophy is attractive.

Another countercase:

> The true child job may be “earn/spend money” or “get recognition,” with autonomy-development only a parent/founder aspiration.

This must be separated through observed behavior rather than assumed.
