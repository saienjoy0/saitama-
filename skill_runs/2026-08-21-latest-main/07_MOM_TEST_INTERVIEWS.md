# Mom Test Interview Guide — Latest Main Spec

Applied skill: `mom-test`

Goal: validate the parent/child problem and current behavior **before** selling the mechanism. Avoid “Would you use it?”, “Do you like this?”, and hypothetical willingness-to-pay questions.

## Parent — primary interview

### Learning goal 1: Does the autonomy/coaching problem occur in real life?
- “Think of the last time you wanted your child to notice or do something without you telling them. What happened?”
- “What did you say first?”
- “At what point did you step in?”
- “Why did you step in then?”
- “What happened afterward?”
- “How often has something like that happened in the last two weeks?”

### Learning goal 2: What do they already do?
- “How are chores/allowance/household responsibilities handled now?”
- “What have you tried to get them to act without repeated reminders?”
- “What did you keep doing? What did you stop?”
- “Have you paid for any app,教材,習い事, or tool specifically because you wanted them to become more independent? Walk me through the last one.”
- “When you feel ‘I’ll just do it myself’, what usually caused that?”

### Learning goal 3: Is the problem strong enough for commitment?
Do not ask “would you pay?” first.

After problem discovery, offer a real next step:
- “We are running a 3–5 session household test. It means using one real situation each time and allowing us to observe prompts/interventions. Would you like to choose an actual first day now?”
- At the end of a successful pilot, make a concrete paid continuation offer and record the decision.
- “Who else do you know who struggles with this enough that you’d introduce us?”

## Child — age-appropriate behavioral interview

Do not explain the product first.

- “Tell me about the last time you helped someone at home without being told exactly what to do.”
- “How did you notice?”
- “What made you decide to do something?”
- “Was there a time you wanted to help but didn’t know what to do?”
- “What did the adult say?”
- “When you get stuck, what kind of help makes it easier? What kind feels like they just told you the answer?”
- “Tell me about the last time you said ‘I don’t want to’ or changed how you were going to do a job. What happened?”
- “Last time you got money or a reward for helping, what did you do with it?”

Avoid asking whether an AI character sounds fun. Show only after baseline behavior is understood.

## After a real session — debrief

For child:
- “At what point did it feel like your idea?”
- “Was there a point where the app/adult gave too much help?”
- “What would you have done if nobody asked you the first question?”

For parent:
- “When did you feel tempted to give the answer?”
- “What did you actually say/do?”
- “Was this less or more work than the way you usually handle it?”
- “What would have stopped you from doing this a second time?”

These are post-behavior questions; still avoid fishing for compliments.

## Grandparent — secondary, only after core parent-child signal

- “Tell me about the last time your grandchild asked you for advice/help or showed you something they did.”
- “How did that conversation start?”
- “What do you currently do to stay involved?”
- “What makes you reach out first, and what stops you?”

Do not let grandparent interest become evidence for the child-autonomy core unless it causes measurable next child behavior.

## Signals to capture

Strong:
- specific recent examples;
- existing workaround/time/money spent;
- real pilot booking;
- repeat session;
- referral introduction;
- paid continuation/deposit.

Weak:
- “great idea”;
- “my child would love this”;
- hypothetical price answers;
- feature requests without underlying problem;
- compliments after seeing a polished demo.

## Segmentation clue

If interviews scatter, slice by households where the parent recently attempted to change the child’s independent behavior and has already spent time/money trying to solve it. A useful initial segment must be findable, not merely demographic.
