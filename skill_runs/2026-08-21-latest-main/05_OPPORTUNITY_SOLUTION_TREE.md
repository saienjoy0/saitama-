# Opportunity Solution Tree — Latest Main Spec

Applied skill: `opportunity-solution-tree`

**Evidence warning:** The current materials are rich product/design artifacts but not sufficient primary customer research. Therefore the opportunities below are provisional and should be replaced/reshaped by interviews and observed household sessions.

## Desired outcome

**Autonomous transfer success rate:** proportion of pilot child-parent pairs that, within a repeated-session test, satisfy the project’s three conditions together:
1. child voluntarily returns;
2. combined AI + parent support shows a downward trend;
3. a new household situation is eventually brought/framed by the child with materially less prompting.

This is an early discovery metric, not proof of durable long-term development.

## Opportunity 1 — Child needs a start without losing authorship

Hypothesis from current design: “I do not always know what to notice or how to start, but if you tell me the answer it stops being mine.”

### Solutions
1. AI-apprentice conversation that asks the child to explain/correct.
2. Non-AI observation card (“notice repeated searching/waiting/rushing”).
3. One-tap choice of observation lens followed by free response.
4. Human concierge using the same controlled hint ladder.

### Experiments
- Compare first-session engagement and maximum support intensity across AI-apprentice vs simpler observation scaffold.
- Track which variant produces a child-generated proposal without facilitator supplying the action.

## Opportunity 2 — Parent wants autonomy but cannot coach every step

Hypothesis: “I want my child to think for themselves, but in daily life I give the answer because it is faster.”

### Solutions
1. Parent enters only a situation, not a task.
2. Product handles graded questions while parent only sets safety/house rules.
3. One-tap recipient feedback (“time saved / easier / happy”) plus optional text.
4. Parent intervention reminder: “don’t answer yet; let them think” only when necessary.

### Experiments
- Mom Test interviews about the last real episode of stepping in.
- Concierge pilot measuring parent active minutes/interventions and whether parent returns for session 2/3.

## Opportunity 3 — Scaffolding must not become a new dependency

Hypothesis: “If the app always tells me what to look for, I may only act when prompted.”

### Solutions
1. Step-specific support profile.
2. Hint ladder from free response → reframed question → observation lens → options → one example.
3. Fading start screen from concrete observation prompt → “anything you noticed?” → open add button/no prompt.
4. Periodic “no prompt” sessions to test transfer.

### Experiments
- Same-child longitudinal sessions; record maximum support intensity by step.
- Introduce a new household context and test whether the child transfers the process without a familiar cue.

## Opportunity 4 — Family feedback must feel like receiving value, not grading

Hypothesis: “I want to know whether I actually helped, not whether I passed.”

### Solutions
1. Recipient chooses what changed (time/ease/joy/information/etc.).
2. Optional short voice/text response.
3. No pass/fail; allow discussion/redo only if naturally needed.

### Experiments
- Compare completion/next-action behavior under pass/fail wording vs concrete-value wording in a small prototype.
- Interview child about which feedback felt informative vs evaluative.

## Opportunity 5 — Parent may value progress but product success can reduce usage

Hypothesis: “I may pay for my child gaining independence, even if the goal is to need less support later.”

### Solutions
1. Time-bounded “growth cycle” / course rather than infinite engagement model.
2. Periodic new-domain modules or family challenge cycles.
3. Parent-facing evidence of support reduction/progress, not streaks.
4. Family/education program model where graduation is an explicit outcome.

### Experiments
- After pilot, offer a concrete finite paid continuation instead of an endless subscription.
- Test whether parents value “graduation / less support needed” as a benefit rather than a churn problem.

## Priority

Until primary research exists, prioritize Opportunities 1–3 because they directly determine whether the transfer mechanism can function. Opportunity 2 also determines whether the leading payer/requester has a real problem. Do not build Opportunity 5 business-model machinery before real household value appears.
