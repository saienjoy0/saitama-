## What changed in the input

Compared with the prior mainline spec, the latest spec now explicitly includes:

- scaffolding research concepts: contingency / fading / transfer of responsibility, with an explicit caveat that household effectiveness is not proven;
- a competition correction: problem discovery, child proposals, ingenuity, and reflection already appear in competing products such as `おてつだいステップUP`, so “problem discovery starts earlier” cannot stand alone as differentiation;
- an updated differentiation hypothesis centered on **progressive transfer of control** rather than one isolated feature;
- a 3-part PoC success definition treated as an AND condition: repeat desire × declining total support × spontaneous real-life problem bringing;
- parent + AI **total support**, not AI hints alone, as the relevant support metric;
- a parent problem hypothesis: parents may want independent judgment but lack time/patience to repeatedly design the right questions and wait for children to think;
- a sharper warning that lower app usage may itself be a successful learning outcome, creating a business-model tension that must be addressed rather than hidden.

## Combined conclusion from the skills

The project has moved past “which features should the app have?” and past “is problem discovery unique?”.

The current load-bearing product bet is now:

> Can a household experience be enjoyable enough to repeat while the **combined support from AI + adults actually decreases**, and can responsibility transfer far enough that the child starts noticing and bringing new real-life situations without being prompted?

The current load-bearing business bet is separate:

> Is the parent-side problem painful and frequent enough that households will repeatedly facilitate the experience and ultimately make a real commitment to pay, even though a successful child may need the product less over time?

## Highest-priority unknowns after this run

1. **Transfer mechanism:** total support can fall over repeated sessions without the child simply failing or the parent taking over.
2. **Repeat desire:** children voluntarily opt into another attempt while support is fading.
3. **Cross-situation transfer:** children begin to notice/bring a new household situation without a prompt, not merely repeat a memorized chore.
4. **Parent pain + burden:** the proposed parent problem exists in real households, and the product reduces rather than relocates the coaching burden.
5. **Commercial commitment:** after experiencing value, a concrete paid continuation obtains real commitment.
6. **Autonomy integrity:** refusal/modification/negotiation remain real within safe family boundaries.

## Recommended immediate sequence

1. Run a 3–5 session longitudinal concierge pilot with the same child-parent pair.
2. Record **maximum support intensity per cognitive step** plus parent interventions, not just AI message count.
3. Compare the AI-apprentice onboarding with at least one simpler non-AI or low-AI scaffold.
4. Observe whether a new situation is brought in by the child without prompting.
5. Interview parents using past-behavior questions before pitching the product.
6. After experienced value, make a specific paid-continuation offer and record actual commitment/rejection.
7. Re-score the assumption map from evidence and kill/modify the implementation hypothesis if the AND-condition does not move.
