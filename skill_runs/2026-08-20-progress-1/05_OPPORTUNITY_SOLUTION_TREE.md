# Opportunity Solution Tree — rerun with 今の進捗(1)

Applied upstream skill: `opportunity-solution-tree`.

## Desired outcome
A child increasingly initiates real household problem discovery and value proposals with less adult/AI scaffolding, while the family repeats the interaction with acceptable burden.

## Opportunity 1 — Child does not know what to notice
### Solutions
- AI apprentice asks the child to teach it about the household.
- Observation prompt such as “find something someone repeats.”
- Simple visual observation cards without AI.

### Experiments
Compare first-use engagement and independent observations across 3 onboarding variants.

## Opportunity 2 — Child gets stuck at different parts of the loop
### Solutions
- multidimensional support profile;
- simple session-by-session hint history;
- child-selected “I need help here” control.

### Experiments
Track where help is actually needed. Compare whether a complex profile gives better next-session support than a simple last-needed-hint rule.

## Opportunity 3 — Assistance itself can create dependency
### Solutions
- hint ladder using least help first;
- fade observation prompts over time;
- explicit “try without a hint” mode;
- periodic no-prompt sessions.

### Experiments
Measure whether hint requirements decrease and whether children later bring their own observations.

## Opportunity 4 — Parent coaching burden can kill repetition
### Solutions
- parent enters only a situation, not a task;
- one-tap value feedback;
- optional free text only when useful;
- AI organizes family feedback but does not impersonate family.

### Experiments
Measure actual parent minutes/taps per session and whether a second session happens.

## Opportunity 5 — Autonomy can become nominal rather than real
### Solutions
- explicit refuse/modify/negotiate paths;
- family boundary setup before session;
- no punishment/point loss for refusal;
- facilitator observation of real family reaction.

### Experiments
Create a safe opportunity for the child to modify or decline and observe what the adult actually does.

## Opportunity 6 — Value may not feel concrete to child or payer
### Solutions
- family selects what changed: time saved / easier / happy / learned something;
- short verbal feedback;
- optional compensation agreed before action;
- record of decision process, not just completion.

### Experiments
Compare generic praise versus concrete value feedback on reflection and repeat behavior.

## Opportunity 7 — Payer may not value the same thing as the child
### Solutions
- parent-facing progress evidence focused on less prompting and more child-initiated behavior;
- paid pilot only after real sessions;
- no promise that more app usage equals more growth.

### Experiments
After a small sequence of sessions, show parent observed behavior change and offer a specific paid continuation.

## Priority
Do not build all branches. First test Opportunities 1, 3 and 4 together because they determine engagement, autonomy mechanism, and household repeatability.
