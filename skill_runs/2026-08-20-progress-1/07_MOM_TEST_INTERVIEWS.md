# Mom Test Interview Guide — rerun with 今の進捗(1)

Applied upstream skill: `mom-test`.

Goal: learn from past behavior. Do not pitch the app or ask hypothetical liking/willingness alone.

## Parent — problem / current behavior
1. Tell me about the last time you wanted your child to notice something and act without being directly told.
2. What did you actually say or do?
3. What happened when you gave less instruction?
4. When your child gets stuck, how do you usually help?
5. What is the last example where you felt “I gave too much of the answer” or “I had to keep reminding them”?
6. What do you currently use—chore charts, allowance rules, school activities, apps, conversation, nothing?
7. How much time does helping/monitoring this usually take?
8. What is the last thing you paid for because you wanted more independence, initiative, learning habits or life skills for your child?

## Parent — repeat / burden
9. Think of the last educational or family routine you stopped. Why did it stop?
10. What kind of feedback do you realistically give after your child helps—long comments, a word, money, nothing?
11. When has an app/service created more work for you than it saved?
12. Who in the family would actually keep this routine going?

## Child — behavior, not product opinion
Use age-appropriate language and avoid turning the conversation into an interrogation.
1. Tell me about the last time you noticed someone at home having trouble before they asked you.
2. What did you notice first?
3. What did you do next?
4. Did anyone tell you how to do it? What did they say?
5. Tell me about the last time you changed how an adult asked you to do something.
6. Have you ever said “not now” or “I want to do it another way”? What happened?
7. When you do not know what to do, what kind of help makes it easier to think yourself?
8. Tell me about something you started at home without being reminded.

## Support-decay probes
These are now critical because of the new progress document.
- Parent: “Tell me about something your child used to need help with but now starts alone. How did that change happen?”
- Parent: “What did you stop doing once they could handle it?”
- Child: “What is something people used to remind you about, but now you remember yourself?”
- Child: “How did you learn it? What kind of hint helped most?”

## Compensation
Do not ask only “Would money motivate them?” Ask:
- Tell me about the last time you paid allowance for a specific household action.
- How was the amount decided?
- What happened when the child did not want to do it?
- Have you stopped paying for something because it felt too transactional?

## Commitment after actual test
Only after a real pilot:
- Would you schedule the second session now?
- Would you introduce another household that has the same problem?
- Here is the concrete paid continuation; would you continue under these terms?

## Evidence to capture
Separate:
- observed/past behavior;
- current workaround;
- cost/time/burden;
- specific quotes;
- real commitments;
- product ideas/opinions (lower confidence).

Compliments such as “this is a great idea” are not validation.
