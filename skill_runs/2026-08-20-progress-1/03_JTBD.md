# JTBD — rerun with 今の進捗(1)

Applied upstream skill: `thinking-jobs-to-be-done`.

These remain hypotheses until direct user evidence exists.

## Primary child job
**When** adults usually decide what needs doing and I am not sure what to notice or how to help,
**I want to** notice something real around me, make sense of it, and decide my own way to help,
**so I can** feel that my idea and action genuinely changed something for someone without simply following instructions.

### Desired outcomes
- less dependence on adults/AI for the next step;
- more self-generated observations/proposals;
- confidence that refusal/modification/negotiation are real options;
- visible connection between action and value created.

### Competing alternatives
- direct parental instruction;
- ordinary chore charts / allowance apps;
- school inquiry/free-research activities;
- unstructured real-life learning without an app;
- AI simply giving task ideas;
- doing nothing / non-consumption.

## Parent job
**When** I want my child to become more self-directed but daily life is busy,
**I want to** create opportunities for the child to think and act without having to teach every step,
**so I can** support growth without adding a heavy coaching burden to family life.

### Parent outcomes
- minimal setup and feedback effort;
- child initiates more without repeated reminders;
- evidence of thinking/negotiation, not only completed chores;
- no need to write long comments or constantly grade;
- safe boundaries remain clear.

## Grandparent job (secondary / later)
**When** I want to participate meaningfully in the child’s growth from a distance or across generations,
**I want to** respond with questions, experience, requests, encouragement or optional compensation,
**so I can** contribute to the child’s next action rather than only receive photos.

## strongest countercase
The real job may simply be “parents want children to help more at home.” If direct chore assignment solves that cheaply and effectively, the proposed autonomy-development job may be too weak to pay for. This must be tested, not assumed.

A second countercase: the child’s real job may be “have fun / earn money,” with autonomy growth being mainly an adult interpretation. If so, the child-facing experience and payer proposition may diverge sharply.

## evidence gaps
Need actual interviews/observations on:
- situations where parents deliberately avoid giving the answer;
- children’s reaction to progressively reduced prompts;
- whether parents perceive reduced prompting as valuable;
- current workarounds for building autonomy at home;
- what parents already spend time or money on for this goal.
