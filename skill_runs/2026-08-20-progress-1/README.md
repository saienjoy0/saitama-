## What changed from the previous run
The previous run correctly prioritized first-use repeat desire, parent repeat value, child comprehension, payer commitment, and real autonomy.

The new progress document makes a further mechanism explicit:

> **Support should be targeted to the exact point of difficulty and should fade as the child becomes capable.**

Therefore the business now has two equally important behavioral tests:

1. **Return:** Does the child voluntarily want another attempt?
2. **Release:** Across attempts, can external scaffolding reduce and be replaced by child-initiated noticing/proposals?

High repeat use alone is not sufficient. If a child keeps using the service but always needs the AI to supply the observation theme, options, and next step, the product may create engagement without achieving its stated autonomy mechanism.

Likewise, lower app usage alone is not proof of growth. The product must distinguish:
- less usage because the child became self-directed, from
- less usage because the child became bored or the family stopped using it.

## Strongest current product thesis
This is increasingly better framed as:

**A household inquiry system that gives only the minimum scaffolding a child needs, then intentionally removes that scaffolding as the child becomes able to notice problems, propose value, negotiate, act and reflect independently.**

AI apprentice, support-profile implementation, exact hint-ladder steps and screen wording remain replaceable hypotheses.

## Most dangerous assumptions now
1. Child wants to repeat.
2. Child can perform the loop with minimal help.
3. Required help can measurably decrease across sessions.
4. Reduced help becomes self-initiation rather than dropout.
5. Parent burden stays low enough for repetition.
6. Parent genuinely values the outcome enough to pay.
7. Household power dynamics allow real refusal/modification/negotiation.

## Immediate test
Do not build the support-profile engine yet.

Run 3–5 manual sessions per child and record, by dimension, the minimum hint needed. Also record whether the child later brings a real observation without being prompted.

The first useful evidence table should look like:

| Session | Notice | Articulate | Solution | Negotiate | Reflect | Child-initiated situation? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | No |
| 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | No |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | Yes/No |

Use the actual hint ladder as observational notation, not as a product feature commitment.

## Commercial interpretation
A stronger parent-facing promise may eventually be less about “the child completes more chores” and more about **“you have to tell them less over time, while they start noticing and proposing more themselves.”**

That wording is still a hypothesis until parent interviews and real behavior support it.
