## Core purpose
Move the child from being given work by adults toward independently noticing situations, interpreting what is happening, thinking how they could help, proposing value to family, negotiating conditions, acting, receiving concrete feedback/value, reflecting, and generating the next action.

## Non-negotiable design principles
- Autonomy is gradual and multidimensional: choose, refuse, modify, negotiate, decide method, execute, observe outcome, reflect, improve, discover problems, propose work, make reverse requests.
- Value means a meaningful change for another person, not only productivity or money.
- Household life is treated as a small inquiry loop: notice → think → try → deliver value → observe → reflect → next.
- Do not start with complete freedom; provide only the minimum scaffolding needed, then reduce it.
- Reduced AI/app dependence can be a successful outcome.
- AI should scaffold thinking without supplying the answer or becoming the protagonist.
- Parents/family should not become teachers who constantly assign, hint, grade, and supervise.
- Family feedback should be lightweight; long comments are not mandatory.

## Current implementation hypotheses
### A. AI apprentice + household exploration
AI is a trainee who knows little about the household. The child is the household expert/teacher. Parent supplies a situation worth exploring, not a finished task.

### B. Support profile
Do not give the child one autonomy level. Track where support may be needed across: noticing, problem articulation, solution generation, negotiation, proposal, reflection.

### C. Hint ladder
Increase help stepwise instead of immediately giving an answer:
free response → reframe question → observation lens → options → one example.

### D. Fading entry screen
Initial: concrete observation prompt.
Middle: broad question such as “Anything you noticed today?”
Later: child brings in their own observation without a prompt.

## Parent/family role
- Surface situations worth exploring when needed.
- Set safety/household boundaries.
- Agree on content, conditions and optional compensation.
- Receive actual value created by the child.
- Return lightweight feedback such as thanks or simple “time saved / easier / happy”.
- Avoid designs that create too much parent burden.
