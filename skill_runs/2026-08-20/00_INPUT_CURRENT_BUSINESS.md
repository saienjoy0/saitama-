## Top-level purpose

The service aims to help children grow from people who are merely given tasks by adults into people who can notice situations around them, identify where they may be able to help, think about what value they can create, propose an action to family members, agree on conditions, execute, see the result, reflect, and improve the next action.

The end goal is not chore management, allowance management, financial education, grandparent communication, photo sharing, or AI usage by itself.

## Definition of autonomy used by the business

Autonomy is treated as a gradual transfer of decision-making power:

choose
→ refuse
→ change the content
→ negotiate conditions
→ decide the method
→ execute
→ see the result
→ reflect
→ change the next approach
→ notice a nearby problem
→ propose a job
→ make requests to parents or grandparents

The long-term success state is that the child can increasingly think and act without being told the answer by adults or AI.

## Core loop

Family daily life
→ child notices something / adult gives an entry point
→ child thinks about what is happening
→ child considers what they can do
→ child chooses, modifies, or proposes a job
→ method, deadline, support, and optional compensation are discussed
→ child executes
→ value reaches the recipient
→ family explains concretely what changed or helped, and compensation may be given
→ child decides how to use or save earned money when money is involved
→ child reflects
→ family or grandparents respond with questions, experience, or a next request
→ next observation/action begins

## Design principles

- Do not start with complete freedom and no support.
- Adults or AI may provide situations, options, observation viewpoints, and hints.
- Reduce support as the child gains experience.
- Do not lock advanced children into beginner stages.
- Reward is optional consideration for value delivered, not payment for obedience.
- The child may refuse without punishment or point loss.
- Photos are one reporting method, not a pass/fail submission system.
- Family feedback should state specifically what became easier, faster, happier, clearer, or otherwise valuable.
- Reflection should be short and help the child modify future behavior.
- AI is a scaffold, not the protagonist and not a replacement for family interaction.
- A reduction in AI support can count as success.
- Grandparents are participants who can respond, ask, share experience, or create the next action; they are not only viewers.
- The required record is the child's decision-making and growth process, not a specific “family newspaper” format.

## Current leading onboarding hypothesis

### AI apprentice + household exploration

AI is framed as a novice that does not know the household. The child is the person who knows the household better and teaches the AI what is happening.

The parent should ideally enter only a situation worth exploring, such as “before dinner is busy,” rather than a finished task such as “set chopsticks for 100 yen.”

The intended mechanism is that explaining and correcting the AI causes the child to observe the household and verbalize the problem before proposing an action.

This is an implementation hypothesis, not a proven design.

## Most important unresolved question

What onboarding causes a first-time child, after roughly 3–5 minutes, to want to do it again?

The AI-apprentice approach is currently the leading hypothesis but remains unvalidated.
