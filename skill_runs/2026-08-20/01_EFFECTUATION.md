## affordable_loss

The source materials do not specify a founder-approved hard money/time cap. Therefore the following is a **proposed experiment cap**, not a confirmed fact:

- Time: one short discovery cycle before a full build decision.
- Money: no custom development outsourcing and no large paid acquisition before the top assumptions are tested.
- Reputation: private or small invited household tests rather than a broad public launch.
- Data/privacy: collect only the minimum child/family information necessary for the experiment and avoid persistent location tracking or unnecessary child media.
- Scope: do not build payment infrastructure, rankings, regional partnerships, or a full family-newspaper system before the core loop is shown to work.

Founder should later replace these proposed caps with explicit numerical caps.

## next_action

Run a **manual/concierge onboarding test** of the core loop before building the application.

Use one real household situation supplied by a parent, then let the child go through a lightweight version of:

situation
→ observe
→ explain what is happening
→ think of what might help
→ choose/modify/propose an action
→ agree conditions
→ act
→ receive specific “what changed” feedback
→ short reflection

The first version can be conducted with a simple chat/prototype plus a human behind the scenes. The purpose is to learn whether the loop is engaging and understandable, not to validate the technical stack.

## commitments_sought_or_won

Seek commitments rather than positive opinions:

1. **Parent commitment:** agrees to supply a real household situation and spend time in an actual test.
2. **Child behavioral commitment:** voluntarily starts or continues the activity, rather than merely saying the idea sounds fun.
3. **Repeat commitment:** household agrees to use it again on another day/situation.
4. **Payer commitment:** after experiencing the test, parent accepts a concrete paid-pilot offer or other real financial commitment.
5. **Referral commitment:** parent introduces another household because they believe the test is worth someone else's time.

No commitment should be inferred from compliments alone.

## contingencies_used

Treat surprises as new means:

- If children enjoy teaching AI but do not reach problem discovery, keep the interaction pattern but change the prompts.
- If children dislike AI conversation but enjoy observation/negotiation, remove AI from the foreground and preserve the core loop.
- If parents over-specify the answer, redesign parent intake around situations or observable context rather than tasks.
- If payment weakens the interaction, test non-monetary recognition or only selective paid jobs while preserving the child’s right to negotiate.
- If grandparents naturally create stronger repeat behavior than parents, move them earlier in the loop; if not, keep them later.

## emerging_goal

The near-term goal should not be “build the app.”

It should be:

> Find a repeatable household interaction in which a child can move from being shown a situation to independently noticing, proposing, negotiating, and delivering value — and identify the adult who will repeatedly pay or commit resources for that change.

## stop_or_loop

Continue under effectuation until either:

- real repeat use and a payer commitment appear,
- the leading onboarding hypothesis is falsified and a stronger variant emerges,
- or repeated tests show that the core child-behavior change is not attractive or understandable enough to justify continuing.

Switch to conventional execution planning only after the primary user, payer, core interaction, and minimum repeatable loop are sufficiently stable.
