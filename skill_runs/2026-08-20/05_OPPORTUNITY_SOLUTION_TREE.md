# Opportunity Solution Tree — Provisional

Applied existing skill: `skills/vendor/phuryn_pm-skills/opportunity-solution-tree/SKILL.md`

## Evidence status

The current project has a strong product thesis but not yet enough customer-research evidence to call the following branches validated “opportunities.” Therefore they are **hypothesis opportunities** to be confirmed or rejected through interviews and observation.

## Desired Outcome

**Primary product outcome to improve:**

> Increase the share of real household situations in which the child independently notices or interprets what is happening, proposes or meaningfully modifies an action, and completes the interaction with less adult/AI answer-giving than before.

### Measurement candidates
- child-initiated problem observations per session
- child-generated or child-modified proposals per session
- proportion of sessions using refusal/negotiation/modification appropriately
- number of answer-giving interventions by adult/AI
- repeat attempts initiated voluntarily by the child
- change in support level across repeated sessions

A baseline and numerical target must be established through first observations; they are not yet evidence-backed.

# Tree

## OUTCOME

**Child independently notices, proposes, negotiates, and delivers value with progressively less scaffolding**

### Opportunity O1 — “I do not know what to notice.”

Hypothesis: ordinary household life is too unstructured for a child to immediately identify where value could be created.

#### Solution candidates
1. **AI apprentice framing** — child teaches an AI novice what is happening in the household.
2. **Situation-only parent prompt** — parent enters “before dinner is busy” rather than a finished task.
3. **Observation cards/prompts** — simple prompts such as “Who is waiting?”, “What is repeated?”, “What looks difficult?”, “What is someone searching for?”

#### Experiments
- Compare three onboarding variants on the same type of household situation.
- Measure time to first child-generated observation, number of hints, and voluntary repeat desire.

---

### Opportunity O2 — “When adults show me the situation, they also give me the answer.”

Hypothesis: parent behavior can unintentionally collapse the discovery step and return the experience to conventional chore assignment.

#### Solution candidates
1. Parent input form that accepts only a situation/context first.
2. Parent micro-coaching that rewrites task commands into observation prompts.
3. Manual facilitator intercepts over-specified parent input during PoC.

#### Experiments
- Ask parents to submit real situations without training, then observe how often they give the answer.
- Test whether a short rewrite/coaching step reduces finished-task inputs.

---

### Opportunity O3 — “Too much freedom is confusing; too much guidance feels like a quiz.”

Hypothesis: the right level of scaffolding differs by age and experience.

#### Solution candidates
1. Three-tier hint system: observation cue → category cue → example only as last resort.
2. Choice between open response and optional buttons.
3. Adaptive reduction of hints after successful attempts.

#### Experiments
- Track how many prompts each child needs to produce a proposal.
- On repeat sessions, deliberately reduce one support layer and observe whether performance and enjoyment remain.

---

### Opportunity O4 — “I can choose, but the choice may not be real.”

Hypothesis: child autonomy can become superficial if refusal or negotiation is not accepted in the real family interaction.

#### Solution candidates
1. Explicit “not now / change it / propose another” paths.
2. Pre-agreed family rule: refusal has no punishment or point loss.
3. Negotiation step for time, method, help, deadline, and optional compensation.

#### Experiments
- During a pilot, observe what happens when the child actually refuses or changes a condition.
- Interview parent and child separately afterward about whether the choice felt real.

---

### Opportunity O5 — “I did the thing, but I do not know what value I created.”

Hypothesis: generic praise (“good job”) does not teach the child to connect action to recipient value.

#### Solution candidates
1. Recipient prompt: “What became easier / faster / clearer / happier?”
2. Before/after value reflection with one concrete sentence.
3. Voice response from family instead of a score/pass-fail button.

#### Experiments
- Compare generic praise versus specific value feedback in successive trials.
- Ask the child afterward what they believe changed for the recipient.

---

### Opportunity O6 — “Reflection feels like homework.”

Hypothesis: reflection is necessary for learning but can reduce repeat desire if it is heavy.

#### Solution candidates
1. One-question reflection (“Next time, what would you change?”).
2. Voice answer capped at a short response.
3. Optional choice cards plus one free-text/voice field.

#### Experiments
- Compare no reflection, one-question reflection, and multi-question reflection on completion time and repeat desire.

---

### Opportunity O7 — “Parent sees developmental value but not enough value to keep paying or facilitating.”

Hypothesis: the parent needs a visible reason to continue beyond one fun interaction.

#### Solution candidates
1. Simple progress view showing reduced hints and increased child-initiated proposals.
2. Weekly summary of decisions/negotiations/value delivered rather than a photo album.
3. Paid pilot that includes facilitator support and progress interpretation.

#### Experiments
- After the parent sees real sessions, present a specific paid continuation offer.
- Compare which progress evidence the parent actually uses when explaining why they would continue.

## Priority opportunities for the next cycle

Because the current business has not yet validated customer opportunities, do not attempt to build all seven branches.

### Priority 1: O1 — child does not know what to notice
This directly tests the leading onboarding hypothesis.

### Priority 2: O3 — balance freedom and scaffolding
This is necessary for the autonomy mechanism to work for first-time users.

### Priority 3: O7 — parent continuation/payment value
This connects the child experience to commercialization.

O2 and O4 should be observed during the same tests because they can invalidate the core even if the child UI works.
